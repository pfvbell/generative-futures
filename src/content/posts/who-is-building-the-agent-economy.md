The early internet was built, broadly, as a neutral commons before anyone monetised it. The agent economy is being built the other way round, by the firms and states that intend to own it, before most people know it exists.

This essay maps that construction across three layers: how agents *talk* (communication protocols), how they *pay* (the most consequential layer), and how they *prove who they are* (identity). In each, a US model of open, competing standards is running up against a Chinese model of vertical integration that makes open protocols unnecessary. The choices being locked in now, about payments rails and identity credentials, will shape rent extraction, surveillance, and network power for a long time.

It's a concrete case study in the project's core claim: infrastructure is politics, and the window to shape it is open only briefly.
