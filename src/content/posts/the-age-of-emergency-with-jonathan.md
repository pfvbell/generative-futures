AI debates are soaked in urgency, race dynamics, decisive advantages, no time to think. This episode steps back to ask what that constant sense of emergency is doing to our politics.

Jonathan White calls it "temporal claustrophobia": the feeling that the future is closing in, that there's no time, that we must act *now*. He argues this mood is less a measure of how bad things really are than a symptom of weak institutions, and that it's dangerous, because emergency conditions are precisely where the far right thrives, since fascism needs no coherent plan, only motion.

His alternative is a democratic politics pitched at the right temporal "sweet spot", decades, not the millennia of effective-altruist long-termism, nor the next news cycle. It's a bracing frame for thinking about how we should hold time when talking about AI.
