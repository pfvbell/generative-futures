Predictions that machines will replace teachers are over a century old, Edison thought film would do it, and they keep being wrong. This essay explains why, and what AI will actually change.

The argument rests on three things automation struggles with. First, humans are biologically built to learn *from other humans*: the teacher-student relationship awakens attention and motivation in a way a screen doesn't. Second, school is where we acquire the social skills the labour market increasingly rewards. Third, and most practically, the real work of schools lives in tacit, undocumented knowledge, the stuff in teachers' heads that was never written down for an AI to learn from.

None of this means AI is irrelevant. Following the now-standard task framework, the essay argues AI will automate *tasks*, marking, planning, admin, and in doing so could help with a looming global shortage of millions of teachers. Augmentation, not replacement.
