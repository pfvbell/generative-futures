This is one of the Tech Futures Project newsletters, a roundup of the most useful recent research on AI and the economy, filtered through the publication's political-economy lens.

The standout is an OpenAI study that actually measured model performance against experts across 44 occupations, with results that scramble the usual intuitions: AI already beats specialists in several white-collar fields, yet stumbles on industrial engineering and nursing, and, oddly, excels at the work of a private detective. The lesson is that "which jobs are safe?" has a far messier answer than the headlines suggest.

It also surfaces two pieces of serious labour economics: a research agenda for what would even *count* as "transformative" AI, and Pascual Restrepo's unsettling argument about the paths by which AI could push wages toward zero. A good primer if you want the economics, not the vibes.
